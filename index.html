Projects

Spelling Letters Through Brainwaves

This paper explores the potential of brain-computer interfaces (BCIs), a key area within human-computer interaction (HCI), by using non-invasive EEG technology to detect letters from brain signals. Although BCI research is still rare in Mongolia, this project represents a small, early attempt to connect brain signals with technology. While it's just a starting point, it hopes to contribute to the foundation for future developments in the field.

Smart Cane For Visually Impaired People

This paper introduces an early version of a smart cane designed to assist visually impaired individuals by addressing common challenges such as detecting water, obstacles, and holes. The system uses five infrared sensors and a water sensor, all managed by a microcontroller, to provide real-time audio feedback. Developed in response to limited infrastructure and slow IoT adoption, this project serves as a small step toward improving accessibility and everyday mobility.

Location Sensing Smart Backpack

This paper presents a smart backpack created to support visually impaired individuals as they navigate dynamic urban environments. With functions like location sensing, time announcements, and location sharing, the device aims to improve safety, independence, and daily usability. As an early-stage effort, it hopes to contribute to more inclusive and adaptable solutions for users facing evolving urban challenges.
